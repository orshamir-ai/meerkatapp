Privacy Policy

Draft — not yet in force.

This is a working draft, not a published privacy policy.

The sections below describe what the app has been built to do, taken from the application's own source and documentation. They have not been reviewed by a lawyer, they are not a legal commitment, and they must be replaced with a reviewed policy before Meerkat Universe is submitted to any app store.

Who this is for

Meerkat Universe is intended for children. The app is built to the requirements of the Apple App Store Kids Category, and the notes below reflect the decisions that follow from that.

What the app is built to collect

No advertising, and no third-party analytics

The app contains no advertising and no third-party analytics SDK. There is no behavioural profiling of a player and no data broker relationship. This is enforced in the build itself: an automated check reads the app's dependency list and fails if a blocked package is ever added.

Game progress

Progress — the level reached, beetles earned and spent, purchased items and capacity — is stored on the device. When a player signs in with an Apple or Google account, that progress is also stored in the app's own cloud so it survives a lost or replaced device.

Playing with friends

A player who signs in and opens the social hub publishes a small profile so other players can find them: a chosen nickname, a friend code, the level they have reached, and a chosen picture from a fixed set. There is no free text, no photo upload, and no message field anywhere in the app.

Crash reports

When the app crashes, a diagnostic report is sent so the fault can be fixed. It records what the software was doing — the app version, the screen, the level — and never who was playing. No nickname, friend code or account identifier is attached to a crash report.

Product measurement

The app records a small number of events about how the game itself is going — a round started, a round finished, an item bought. These go to the app's own server, not to a third-party analytics service. The events a build may send are restricted by a fixed list written into the app, and the app sends no player identifier with them at all.

Things this draft does not yet state

A published policy will also need to cover, at minimum: the legal entity responsible, the legal basis for processing, retention periods, the specific sub-processors used, how a parent may make a request about their child's data, and the contact route for doing so. None of those are stated here because none of them have been decided and recorded.
